When we decided to build Dog and Bone, one of the first and most important decisions we faced was which physical device to build on. Get this wrong, and everything else falls apart.

We had a clear set of requirements:

1. **Android** (not iOS — Apple's operating system doesn't allow the level of configuration we needed)
2. **Affordable** — the phone should be accessible, not a premium purchase
3. **Durable** — it should survive everyday life
4. **Good camera** — because people genuinely use and value their phone cameras
5. **Good battery life** — long enough to go a day without anxiety about charging
6. **Wide availability** — spare parts, replacement devices, easy to source
7. **Clean Android** — minimal manufacturer bloatware, clear upgrade path

We tested a lot of phones. And we kept coming back to the Samsung Galaxy A12.

## The Specs That Matter

Let's be concrete about what the A12 offers.

**Display:** 6.5-inch PLS TFT LCD, 720 x 1600 pixels. This is not a flagship 4K OLED screen, and that's fine. The display is large, bright, clear, and comfortable to read. For our Senior profile users in particular, the screen size is a genuine advantage. For the Balance profile, the slightly lower resolution and lack of the premium screen quality that makes Instagram photos pop is, if anything, a small feature — the phone is less seductive.

**Processor:** MediaTek Helio P35 octa-core, with 4GB RAM. This is a mid-range processor by the standards of the year it was made, and it is more than adequate for the Dog and Bone use case. Our phones run a stripped-back set of apps without heavy games, social media, or resource-intensive background processes. The A12 runs everything we ship with it smoothly.

**Camera:** Quad camera system — 48MP main, 5MP ultra-wide, 2MP macro, 2MP depth sensor. The 48MP main sensor is genuinely good, particularly in decent light. It produces photographs that most people would be proud of. For the use cases our customers have — capturing family moments, travelling, everyday life — the camera is excellent.

**Battery:** 5000mAh. This is a large battery for a phone in this class, and it translates to real-world longevity. Most users of our Balance and Family profiles get through a full day comfortably. Senior profile users, who typically use the phone less intensively, frequently go two days. Battery anxiety is a real phenomenon — the worry about running out of charge that leads people to spend even more time tethered to a charging point. The A12's battery significantly reduces this.

**Storage:** 64GB base (expandable to 1TB via microSD). More than sufficient for a Dog and Bone configuration, which doesn't need space for large apps, games, or downloaded social media content.

**Build:** Polycarbonate body. This is a plastic phone, not a glass-and-aluminium premium device. We consider this a feature. Polycarbonate is more durable than glass, survives drops better, and doesn't require a case. The A12 is a phone that you can use without worrying about it.

**Connectivity:** 4G LTE, Wi-Fi 802.11 a/b/g/n/ac, Bluetooth 5.0. All the connectivity that matters for 2026 usage. (5G is genuinely not necessary for the use cases our phones are configured for, and the absence of 5G is part of how the A12 achieves its combination of long battery life and affordable price.)

## Why Not a Flagship Device?

This is the question we get asked most often, particularly by people buying the Senior profile for an elderly parent. The instinct to buy the best is understandable, but it's misplaced here.

**Flagship phones are designed to be impressive. Dog and Bone phones are designed to be useful.**

A Samsung Galaxy S25 or an iPhone 16 costs several times more than an A12, has a more powerful processor, a better camera system, and a higher-resolution screen. It also has more features, more complexity, more ways to be distracted, more sensitivity to drops, and a shorter battery life.

For Dog and Bone's use case, **the flagship's advantages are largely irrelevant and its disadvantages are significant.** The extra camera quality is noticeable mainly in professional photography contexts. The extra processing power makes no difference when you're making a phone call. The premium screen makes the social media content we've removed more immersive — not a benefit in our context.

What the A12 lacks in headline specifications, it more than compensates for in durability, battery life, repairability, and price accessibility.

## Why Not a "Dumb Phone"?

The other end of the spectrum is the "dumb phone" approach — basic handsets with number keypads and minimal functionality that are marketed as digital wellness solutions.

We understand the appeal, and for some people these devices are the right answer. But they create genuine practical problems:

- No camera, or a very poor one
- No WhatsApp or Signal — you're isolated from messaging networks that your family and friends use
- No maps or navigation
- No music or podcast apps
- Often poor call quality
- Often difficult to source and service

For people who have professional and social lives in 2026, cutting off these functions creates real inconvenience. Dog and Bone's philosophy is to **remove what harms, not what's useful.**

The A12 gives us the full smartphone capability to build on, and Android gives us the operating system control to configure it precisely the way we need.

## Android as the Platform

Apple's iOS does not permit the level of configuration that Dog and Bone requires. There is no way to prevent app installation, lock down the interface, or configure a simplified launcher at the system level on an iPhone. Apple's walled garden, which is a feature for most users, is a significant constraint for our use case.

Android, and specifically the version of Android that Samsung ships on the A12, gives us the access we need. We can:

- Disable and prevent the reinstallation of specific apps
- Lock down the Play Store with PIN or remove access entirely
- Configure a simplified, custom launcher as the default
- Adjust system settings to be appropriate for each profile
- Prevent settings changes that would undermine the configuration

This level of control is essential to Dog and Bone's value proposition. Without it, the profiles would be cosmetic — easily undone by a determined teenager or an elderly parent who accidentally clicked the wrong thing.

## Availability and Longevity

A final consideration that informed our choice: the A12 is one of the best-selling mid-range phones in history. It has been produced in large quantities, is available worldwide, and has an ecosystem of spare parts, third-party cases, and accessories.

This matters to us because we ship phones to customers who need them to work reliably for years. A phone that can be repaired when the screen cracks, that has accessories readily available, and that will continue to be supported by its manufacturer, is a better choice for our customers than a cutting-edge device that will be obsolete and unsupported in two years.

**Dog and Bone is a long-term choice, not a gadget purchase.** We chose a device that's designed for the long term too.
