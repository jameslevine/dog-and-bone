There is a question that more and more people are asking themselves, usually somewhere around 11pm when they're still on their phone having sworn to themselves they'd be asleep by ten. The question is: _why can't I stop?_

It's a good question. It deserves a serious answer, because it is not primarily about willpower or character. It is about design.

## The Philosophy of Intentional Technology

We use the phrase "intentional technology" a lot at Dog and Bone. It's worth being precise about what we mean.

**Intentional technology** is technology that you use when you choose to use it, for the purposes you choose to use it for, and put down when you're done. It is technology that serves your intentions rather than overriding them.

Most modern smartphones are not intentional technology. They are the opposite: **attentional technology.** Their purpose, from the perspective of the companies that make the operating system and the apps, is to capture and hold your attention as long as possible, redirecting your intentions with each notification, each suggested video, each algorithmic nudge.

The distinction matters because it changes how you think about the problem. If you believe you simply lack the willpower to use your phone sensibly, the solution is to strengthen your willpower — and most "digital wellness" features are designed around this premise. Screen time reports, usage limits, grayscale modes: they are all asking you to resist the phone.

But if you understand that the phone is engineered to defeat your willpower — that the best minds in Silicon Valley have spent decades working out how to override your intentions — then the solution is different. **The solution is to change the phone.**

## What Balance Actually Means

Balance, in the context of technology, doesn't mean equal amounts of phone time and non-phone time. It doesn't mean limiting yourself to two hours a day or scheduling a digital sabbath.

Balance means **alignment between what you intend to do and what you actually do.** A balanced relationship with technology is one where you pick up your phone because you decided to, use it for what you needed, and put it down. Where you are not scrolling something you didn't choose to open. Where your evenings are not colonised by feeds you never subscribed to.

This sounds simple. For most people using a conventional smartphone, it is profoundly difficult to achieve — not because of personal failure, but because the phone is working against them.

The Dog and Bone Balance profile is designed for people who want to live this way: **connected but not consumed.**

## What the Balance Profile Keeps

We are not building phones for hermits. The Balance profile is designed for adults with full, modern lives who need a genuinely functional smartphone.

The Balance profile includes everything that makes a smartphone genuinely useful:

**Communication:** Phone calls, SMS, WhatsApp, Signal. Stay in touch with everyone who matters.

**Navigation:** Google Maps and public transport apps. Get around the world without a printed street map.

**Banking and payments:** Your banking apps, contactless payment. Manage your money on the go.

**Camera:** The Samsung Galaxy A12 has a good camera. Capture your life.

**Music and podcasts:** Streaming music and podcast apps. Listen to what you love.

**Email and calendar:** Manage your professional and personal life.

**News:** A curated news reader, checked on your terms rather than pushed to you via notifications.

**Practical apps:** Weather, calculator, notes, reminders. The quiet tools you actually need.

## What the Balance Profile Removes

The Balance profile does not include:

- **Social media platforms:** Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat, X, Facebook, Threads
- **Short-form video:** YouTube Shorts, Reels, TikTok
- **Algorithmic content feeds:** Any platform built around showing you content you didn't ask for
- **Addictive games:** The category of game designed around variable reward loops and daily habit formation
- **An accessible app store:** You cannot easily reinstall removed apps

This is not censorship and it is not paternalism. It is precisely what you're asking for when you order the Balance profile. You are the one removing these things, on purpose, in advance, because you know that your in-the-moment self will make different choices than your considered self.

## The Ulysses Contract

There's a concept in behavioural economics called a **Ulysses contract.** It comes from the myth of Odysseus, who knew he would be unable to resist the Sirens' song when he heard it — so he asked his crew to tie him to the mast before they sailed past them.

A Ulysses contract is a commitment made by your present self to constrain your future self. It acknowledges that we are not perfectly rational actors in every moment — that emotions, habits, and carefully engineered platforms can override our considered preferences — and it builds a structure to protect our real intentions from our momentary impulses.

**Ordering a Dog and Bone Balance profile is a Ulysses contract.** You are your considered self, ordering a phone that removes what you know your undecided evening self would scroll. You are tying yourself to the mast before you sail past the Sirens.

This is not weakness. It is self-knowledge applied to design. It is what every other domain of life already acknowledges: we don't keep chocolate in the house if we're trying to eat less of it. We set our savings to auto-transfer so we don't spend the money. We put the alarm clock across the room so we can't hit snooze. Structural solutions work better than willpower alone.

## What People Gain

The accounts we receive from Balance profile users are remarkably consistent. The specific details vary — the person is different, the life is different — but the patterns are the same.

**Time returns.** The two, three, four hours that were disappearing into scrolling become available again. People describe rediscovering hobbies they'd abandoned, starting exercise habits, reading books they'd bought and never opened, having longer and better conversations.

**Mental quietness returns.** The ambient noise of the internet — the outrage, the comparison, the feeling that something important is always happening somewhere else — subsides. People describe feeling less anxious, less distracted, less reactive.

**Presence returns.** Being in a room without the gravitational pull of a device constantly competing for attention is, it turns out, a profoundly different experience. Meals are meals. Conversations are conversations. Sleep is sleep.

**Boredom returns.** This sounds like a negative but is not. Genuine boredom — the state of having nothing demanding your attention — is where creativity, reflection, and insight live. A mind that is never bored is a mind that is always being fed. A mind that has some quiet time to wander is a mind that can think.

## Starting

The question is whether you're ready to make the change in a way that will actually stick — rather than another download of a screen time app, another self-imposed rule, another cycle of failure and self-reproach.

The Balance profile is for when you're done trying to resist and ready to redesign.
